# Honest limits

Read this before you rely on the kit.

## What this kit is

A set of 33 plain checks, each tied to a public source, with prompts that let an AI assistant guide you through them. It was written on 2026-11-24 by Onit Tindwani, Sendora.

## What this kit is not

- **Not legal advice.** It does not replace a lawyer. A lawyer who knows telemarketing and your states should review your consent wording, your script and your policy.
- **Not a guarantee.** Passing every check does not mean a call is legal, and failing one does not mean it is illegal.
- **US only.** Other countries have other rules.
- **Not every state.** Check 27 to 29 cover Florida, Oklahoma and Maryland as examples. Other states have their own laws. Texas and Utah were not covered because their official pages could not be opened. California is used only for recording (check 30), through a mirror site.
- **Not a prediction of fines or lawsuits.** The kit contains no lawsuit count and no total of fines. Check 32 shows a worst case sum, which is a ceiling, not a forecast.

## What an AI assistant can and cannot do here

An assistant can:

- read the check files and ask you the right questions;
- review the forms, scripts, lists and settings you show it, and point out what is missing;
- turn your answers into a results sheet, a ranked fix list and a report.

An assistant cannot:

- see your phone carrier's systems, so it cannot verify call signing (check 25) or the carrier's listing in the Robocall Mitigation Database (check 26). You must ask your provider and keep the written answer.
- verify consent records it is not given, or confirm that a consent record is genuine;
- confirm a list provider's claims;
- know about a law or a ruling after the dates in the checks. Its own knowledge may be older than the rules (check 33).

So "any AI assistant can run all 33" means: it can guide a review of your own material and answers. It does not mean it can audit your business without you.

## Where the kit is uncertain

- **Stop requests (check 21).** The FCC's rule on revoking consent has parts with delayed dates, and an amendment dated October 26, 2026 was linked from the rule page but could not be opened. Treat the dates in the check as possibly out of date.
- **AI disclosure (check 13).** A federal proposal exists from 2024. The kit could not confirm it became a final rule. The check is best practice.
- **Consent from a list provider (check 03).** A federal court in January 2025 threw out an FCC rule limiting consent to one seller at a time. How far one person's consent can reach across several sellers is a legal question that has moved. The kit's safe path is proof that names you.
- **Abandoned calls (check 16).** The rules speak about live or sales representatives. How they apply when an AI answers is a question for a lawyer.
- **Florida license (check 27).** The statute says commercial telephone sellers must be licensed. The kit did not read the exemptions.
- **Oklahoma (check 28).** The kit read the enrolled 2022 bill, not the current numbered statute.
- **California recording law (check 30).** Read on a mirror site, not the official one.
- **Records (check 07).** One FTC guide page still said 24 months in a section for other callers. The rule text says five years.

## Data and privacy

The kit contains no real people. All sample rows are made up and labelled SAMPLE. Do not paste real customer data into a chat assistant unless you have checked that tool's data terms. Use masked samples.

## If you find a mistake

Rules change and mistakes happen. Check the source link in the check, compare with the date read, and tell a lawyer. If you maintain a copy of this kit, update the check and its entry in `docs/03-SOURCES.md` and run `python3 tools/validate_kit.py`.
