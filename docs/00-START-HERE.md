# Start here

You are about to use AI to call or text people, or you already do. This kit helps you check, in plain steps, that you have permission to dial each number and that the call itself follows the main US rules.

**Not legal advice. US only. State law varies.**

## The idea in one minute

- To call or text someone with an AI voice, you generally need their consent first. If the call sells something, the rule asks for a signed written consent (an electronic tick counts if it is valid).
- The proof lives with you, not with the AI tool. If you cannot show where a number came from and what the person agreed to, you do not have a defence.
- Most problems start with the list, not the call. That is why checks 01 to 06 come first.

## Your first 15 minutes

1. Open `checks/01-name-the-source.md`, `02-own-lead-magnet-consent-box.md` and `03-bought-list-terms-and-proof.md`. These are the three from the Reel.
2. Copy `templates/lead-source-register.csv` to your own folder and list where your numbers come from.
3. Give your AI assistant this kit and paste `prompts/00-QUICK-START-PROMPT.md`.

## Words you will see

Short explanations are in `docs/04-GLOSSARY.md`. Each check also explains its terms the first time they appear.

## Where the facts come from

Every check lists its source: a US law, an FCC or FTC rule, a court opinion, a state statute or a company policy, with the exact link and the date the kit read it. See `docs/03-SOURCES.md`. Law firm blogs and news stories are not used as sources for any check.
