# Check 27: Florida: check the Florida rules if you call Florida numbers

Group E: State laws, recording and vendors

## What to verify

If any person on your list is in Florida (a Florida area code is presumed to mean a Florida person), you follow Florida's rules on consent, hours, repeat calls and caller ID, and have checked whether you need a Florida license.

## Why it matters

Florida law bars an unsolicited sales call that uses an automated system or a recorded message without prior express written consent, and its definitions cover text messages. It presumes a call to a Florida area code is to a Florida resident. A separate Florida rule bars solicitation calls before 8 a.m. or after 8 p.m. and more than three calls in 24 hours on the same subject. A person can sue for actual damages or $500, whichever is greater, and a court can increase the award up to three times for willful or knowing violations. Texts have an extra step (reply STOP first, 15 days to comply). The same statute says a commercial telephone seller or salesperson must be licensed. Whether you need a license depends on exemptions the kit did not read, so ask a lawyer or the Florida agency.

## How to fix

1. Apply the consent wording from check 04 to every Florida lead.
2. Use the 8 am to 8 pm window and the three calls in 24 hours cap from check 23 for everyone.
3. Ask a lawyer whether you must hold a Florida commercial telephone seller license.
4. Check the Florida 'no sales solicitation calls' list, which the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services keeps, as well as the national registry.
