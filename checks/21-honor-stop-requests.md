# Check 21: Honor every stop request, in any reasonable form, within ten business days

Group C: Do not call lists and stop requests

## What to verify

A person can stop your calls and texts by saying so in any reasonable way (pressing a key, replying stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel or unsubscribe, or a plain sentence), and you act on it, ideally the same day and never later than ten business days.

## Why it matters

The FCC's revocation rule says a called party may revoke consent using any reasonable method, lists stop words for texts as reasonable by themselves, says a request must be honored within a reasonable time not more than ten business days, and bars you from naming one exclusive way to opt out. Another part of the rule (treating a stop in reply to one kind of message as a stop for all robocalls and robotexts from that caller on unrelated matters) was delayed: the FCC's January 6, 2026 order extended the waiver of that part until January 31, 2027. The Cornell page for the rule also linked an amendment published October 26, 2026 (91 FR 67219) that this kit could not open. STATUS MAY HAVE CHANGED: recheck before relying on any date.

## How to fix

1. Treat any stop as a stop to everything from your business (simplest and safest, and it works whatever happens to the delayed part).
2. Make sure your AI tool and your texting tool send stops to your own do not call list the same day.
3. Test: reply STOP to your own text, say 'please stop calling me' on a test call, send an email asking to stop, and see whether each one lands on the list.
4. Recheck the revocation rule's status before every campaign (check 33).
